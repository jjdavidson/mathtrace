# Main Content

Let $X$ and $X'$ be standardized abelian Cayley graphs with Heuberger matrices $M_X=(y_1\ \cdots\ y_r)$ and $M_{X'}$, respectively.
Suppose that $y_j$ belongs to the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the remaining columns of $M_X$.
If $M_{X'}$ is obtained by deleting $y_j$, then $X=X'$.
In particular, deleting a zero column does not change the realized graph.

## Proof

Let $H_X$ and $H_{X'}$ be the subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}^m$ generated by the columns of $M_X$ and $M_{X'}$, respectively.

Every column of $M_{X'}$ is a column of $M_X$, so $H_{X'}\subseteq H_X$.

By assumption, there are integers $a_i$ such that
\[y_j=\sum_{i\neq j}a_i y_i.\]
Therefore, $y_j\in H_{X'}$.
Since $H_{X'}$ contains $y_j$ and every other column of $M_X$, we have $H_X\subseteq H_{X'}$.

Thus, $H_X=H_{X'}$.
By [[sacg.matrix-realization]], the matrices consequently realize the same standardized abelian Cayley graph.
Hence, $X=X'$.
A zero column belongs to the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of the remaining columns, so the final statement follows.
