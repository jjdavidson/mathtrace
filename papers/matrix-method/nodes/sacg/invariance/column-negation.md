# Main Content

Let $X$ and $X'$ be standardized abelian Cayley graphs with Heuberger matrices $M_X$ and $M_{X'}$, respectively.
If $M_{X'}$ is obtained by multiplying one column of $M_X$ by $-1$, then $X=X'$.

## Proof

Write $M_X=(y_1\ \cdots\ y_r)$, and suppose that $M_{X'}$ is obtained by replacing $y_j$ with $-y_j$.
Let $H_X$ and $H_{X'}$ be the subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}^m$ generated by the columns of $M_X$ and $M_{X'}$, respectively.

Every column of $M_{X'}$ belongs to $H_X$, including $-y_j$.
Hence, $H_{X'}\subseteq H_X$.

Conversely, $y_j=-(-y_j)$ belongs to $H_{X'}$, while every other column of $M_X$ is also a column of $M_{X'}$.
Therefore, $H_X\subseteq H_{X'}$.

It follows that $H_X=H_{X'}$.

By [[sacg.matrix-realization]], the matrices consequently realize the same standardized abelian Cayley graph.
Thus, $X=X'$.
