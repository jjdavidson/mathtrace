# Main Content

Let $G$ be a group, let $S\subseteq G$ be symmetric, and let
\[X=\operatorname{Cay}(G,S).\]
Let $G_e=\langle S\rangle$ be the subgroup generated by $S$, and let $X_e$ be the subgraph of $X$ induced by $G_e$.

Then $X_e$ is the connected component containing the identity element $e$, every connected component of $X$ is isomorphic to $X_e$, and
\[\chi(X)=\chi(X_e).\]

## Proof

A vertex is reachable from $e$ by a walk in $X$ if and only if it can be written as a product of elements of $S$.

Since $S$ is symmetric, the set of such products is precisely the subgroup $G_e=\langle S\rangle$.
Therefore, $X_e$ is the connected component containing $e$.

More generally, the connected component containing a vertex $t\in G$ is the subgraph induced by the left coset $tG_e$.
The map
\[tx\mapsto x\]
from this component to $X_e$ is a graph isomorphism: vertices $tx$ and $ty$ are adjacent if and only if
\[tx=tys\]
for some $s\in S$, which is equivalent to $x=ys$.

Thus, every connected component of $X$ is isomorphic to $X_e$.
A proper coloring of $X_e$ can consequently be copied to every connected component of $X$, while restricting any proper coloring of $X$ to $X_e$ gives a proper coloring of $X_e$.
Hence
\[\chi(X)=\chi(X_e).\]

## Remark

When $G$ is infinite, simultaneously choosing representatives for all the left cosets of $G_e$ uses the axiom of choice.
Without the axiom of choice, the equality $\chi(X)=\chi(X_e)$ can fail.

## Use in this paper

This lemma allows the paper to restrict its study of chromatic numbers of Cayley graphs to connected Cayley graphs.
For $X=\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)$, connectedness is equivalent to the condition that $S$ generates $G$.
