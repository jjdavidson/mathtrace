# Main Content

Let $\omega\in\mathbb{C}$ be algebraic, and write

\[
p(x)
=
\operatorname{min}_{\mathbb{Z}}\omega
=
c_dx^d+\cdots+c_1x+c_0.
\]

Let $G_\infty$ be the additive subgroup of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by the nonnegative powers of $\omega$, and define

\[
W
=
\operatorname{Cay}
\bigl(
G_\infty,
\{\pm\omega^n:n\geq0\}
\bigr).
\]

If

\[
\chi(W)\geq4,
\]

then

\[
|p(1)|=|p(-1)|=1.
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
|p(1)|\neq1
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
|p(-1)|\neq1,
\]

then

\[
\chi(W)\leq3.
\]

## Finite approximating graphs

For $m\geq d+1$, let

\[
G_m
=
\left\langle
1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{m-1}
\right\rangle
\]

and define

\[
X_m
=
\operatorname{Cay}
\bigl(
G_m,
\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\dots,\pm\omega^{m-1}\}
\bigr).
\]

Consider the surjective homomorphism

\[
\phi_m\colon\mathbb{Z}^m\longrightarrow G_m
\]

defined by

\[
\phi_m(e_i)=\omega^{i-1}.
\]

For $j\in\{0,\dots,m-d-1\}$, let $v_j\in\mathbb{Z}^m$ be the coefficient vector of $x^jp(x)$, padded with zeros:

\[
v_j
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{j},
c_0,c_1,\dots,c_d,
\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{m-d-1-j}
\end{pmatrix}^{t}.
\]

The integer-relation property from [[cayley.integer-minimal-polynomial]] gives

\[
\ker\phi_m
=
\left\langle
v_0,\dots,v_{m-d-1}
\right\rangle.
\]

Therefore,

\[
M_m
=
\begin{pmatrix}
v_0&v_1&\cdots&v_{m-d-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is a Heuberger matrix for $X_m$, and [[sacg.standardization-isomorphism]] gives

\[
X_m\cong(M_m)^{\mathrm{SACG}}.
\]

## Evaluation at $1$

Every column of $M_m$ has sum

\[
c_0+c_1+\cdots+c_d=p(1).
\]

If $p(1)=0$, then all column sums are even, so [[sacg.colorings.bipartite-criterion]] gives

\[
\chi(X_m)=2.
\]

If $|p(1)|\geq2$, then the gcd of the column sums is $|p(1)|$, so [[sacg.colorings.column-sum-gcd-bound]] gives

\[
\chi(X_m)\leq3.
\]

Thus

\[
|p(1)|\neq1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\chi(X_m)\leq3.
\]

## Evaluation at $-1$

Multiply row $i+1$ of $M_m$ by $(-1)^i$ for each $i\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}$.

By [[sacg.isomorphisms.matrix-isomorphisms]], the resulting matrix represents a graph isomorphic to $X_m$.

The sum of the entries in its column $v_j$ is

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{j+k}c_k
=
(-1)^jp(-1).
\]

Thus the column sums are all equal to either $p(-1)$ or $-p(-1)$.

If $p(-1)=0$, the transformed graph is bipartite.

If $|p(-1)|\geq2$, its column sums have gcd $|p(-1)|$, so it is $3$-colorable.

Consequently,

\[
|p(-1)|\neq1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\chi(X_m)\leq3.
\]

## Passing to the infinite graph

Suppose that $|p(1)|\neq1$ or $|p(-1)|\neq1$.

The preceding arguments show that

\[
\chi(X_m)\leq3
\]

for every sufficiently large $m$.

Every finite subgraph of $W$ is contained in some $X_m$, because it involves only finitely many vertices and finitely many powers of $\omega$.

Thus every finite subgraph of $W$ is $3$-colorable.

By [[graph.de-bruijn-erdos-theorem]], the entire graph $W$ is $3$-colorable.

Taking the contrapositive gives

\[
\chi(W)\geq4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|p(1)|=|p(-1)|=1.
\]

## Open question

The paper leaves open whether this obstruction is ever attained.

Does there exist an algebraic number $\omega\in\mathbb{C}$ for which the Cayley graph generated by the nonnegative powers of $\omega$ is not $3$-colorable?
