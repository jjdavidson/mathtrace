# Main Content

Define $\omega = \frac{5}{8}+\frac{i\sqrt{39}}{8}$.
Since $|\omega|^2 = 1$, each of $1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3$ is a unit vector in the complex plane.

Let $G$ be the additive subgroup of $\mathbb{C}$ generated by these four vectors, and define $W = \operatorname{Cay}\bigl(G,\{\pm1,\pm\omega,\pm\omega^2,\pm\omega^3\}\bigr)$.
Thus $W$ is a subgraph of the [[cayley.unit-distance-plane|unit-distance graph of the plane]].
We will show that $\chi(W)=3$.

## Minimal polynomial

We have $\omega+\overline{\omega}=\frac{5}{4}$ and $\omega\overline{\omega}=1$.
Therefore
\[\operatorname{min}_{\mathbb{Z}}\omega=4x^2-5x+4.\]
In particular, $4-5\omega+4\omega^2=0$ and, after multiplying by $\omega$,
\[4\omega-5\omega^2+4\omega^3=0.\]

## Heuberger matrix

Consider the surjective homomorphism $\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}^4\to G$ defined by $\phi(e_i)=\omega^{i-1}$ for $i = 1,2,3,4$.
By the integer-relation property in [[cayley.integer-minimal-polynomial]], the kernel of $\phi$ is generated by the vectors $(4,-5,4,0)^t$ and $(0,4,-5,4)^t$.

Consequently, [[sacg.standardization-isomorphism]] gives
\[
W \cong
\begin{pmatrix}
    4&0\\
    -5&4\\
    4&-5\\
    0&4
\end{pmatrix}^{\mathrm{SACG}}_X.
\]

Both columns of this matrix have sum $3$.
Therefore, [[sacg.colorings.column-sum-gcd-bound]] gives $\chi(X)\leq3$.
Since the column sums are odd, [[sacg.colorings.bipartite-criterion]] shows that $X$ is not bipartite.
Hence $\chi(W)=\chi(X)=3$.
